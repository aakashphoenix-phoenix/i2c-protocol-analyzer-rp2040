7. What the bytes mean

Target 0x68: DS3231 RTC (configured)
Decoded from these bytes: Read RTC: 18:58:10
Register pointer 0x00 = seconds
  read 0x00 seconds = 0x10 → BCD 10
  read 0x01 minutes = 0x58 → BCD 58
  read 0x02 hours = 0x18 → BCD 18
  read 0x03 day of week = 0x03 → BCD 3
  read 0x04 date = 0x17 → BCD 17
  read 0x05 month = 0x11 → BCD 11
  read 0x06 year = 0x26 → BCD 26
  read 0x07 alarm 1 seconds = 0x00
  read 0x0E control = 0x1C
  read 0x0F control/status = 0x09

9. How this was proven

  1. The analyzer Pico samples SDA and SCL and timestamps every change with its sample clock.
  2. The edges of this packet are taken from that record (nothing interpolated).
  3. A fresh decoder on the PC re-decodes them by the UM10204 rules (START = SDA↓ while SCL=1, bit = SDA on SCL↑, 9th bit = ACK/NACK, STOP = SDA↑ while SCL=1) and the result is compared with the packet as stored (on the RP2040 analyzer: decoded by the Pico firmware itself).
  4. Timing is measured between captured edges and compared with the UM10204 minimum values.
  5. The master is named only from that master's own transfer report.
